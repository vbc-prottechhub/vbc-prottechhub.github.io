Research

Bridging service and innovation to bring cutting-edge proteomics technologies to the Campus Vienna BioCenter.

What is the Proteomics Tech Hub and how can it facilitate innovation at the VBC campus?

Currently, even leading service facilities struggle to implement cutting-edge technologies, due to lack of dedicated personnel, while customers typically request increasingly sophisticated services to answer more and more complex biological questions. Hence, it is essential to provide the necessary resources to keep up with innovations in the field of mass spectrometry and proteomics. Only this will allow research groups at the VBC to remain competitive and continue to thrive and deliver excellent science. The Proteomics Tech Hub, which has recently spun out of the Protein Chemistry Facility, aims to do exactly this. The main responsibility of this Tech Hub shall be to support the Protein Chemistry Facility by developing new methods and helping to establish them for routine analysis thereby ensuring the Protein Chemistry Facility remains at the forefront of proteomics. In close collaboration with the Protein Chemistry Facility, the Tech Hub shall function as a hybrid model between service facility and research group, developing novel methods and protocols to be applied to daunting biological questions in pilot studies together with VBC research groups. These novel methods are then to be published and transferred to the Protein Chemistry Facility to be offered as regular service analysis.

Which technologies will the Proteomics Tech Hub focus on?

With single cell proteomics (SCP) and crosslinking mass spectrometry (XL-MS), the Proteomics Tech Hub currently focuses on two major lines of research and technological innovation both holding great relevance to the VBC research community. While single cell RNA-seq is readily available globally and at the VBC, single cell proteomics would allow to directly analyze the phenotype-responsible players in single cells and would hence provide an invaluable upgrade for VBC scientists in unraveling cellular proteomic heterogeneity. Single cell proteomics also paves the way for limited amount proteomic analyses, in case only dozens or hundreds of cells are available. XL-MS, on the other hand, is a crucial technology to decipher protein structures as well as protein-protein interactions provides invaluable clues to pinpoint mechanistic details of physiologically relevant biological questions. Depending on the demand on campus and the available resources, the focus of the Tech Hub can be shifted towards SCP or XL-MS.

Additional activities

Additional activities of the Tech Hub shall include engagement in conferences and events to scout novel technologies as well as network with the proteomics community especially in the fast-developing SCP field. At the VBC, conferences on SCP have been held by us with > 100 participants featuring leaders in the field of single cell proteomics as well as Alexander Makarov, the inventor of the popular orbitrap mass spectrometer. These activities are planned to be continued, also aiming to establish standardization in the SCP field & to increase visibility of the VBC as a center of excellence for proteomics. For the 2023 edition of our SCP conference in August already 90 participants have registered, among them Matthias Mann, who is arguably the most eminent authority in the field of proteomics with countless awards, patents, two very successful spin-offs and an H index of 255.

Significance and impact for the Campus Vienna BioCenter

“The advance of technology is based on making it fit in so that you don't really even notice it, so it's part of everyday life.” — Bill Gates, Co-founder of Microsoft

The success and high reputation of our Campus is strongly dependent on powerful core facilities that support its scientists with high quality data and that bring know-how for highly specialized techniques. Their technology must be always up to date and fully functional aiming to fully fit in the everyday campus life. However, we still need to notice the huge effort done to permanently ensure highest quality of such scientific services. The field of proteomics is highly dynamic and evolve fast. To keep track with newest developments and even contribute to them we dedicate ourselves to not only improve existing methods but also develop novel techniques that comply with the needs of the campus.

Especially the field of single cell proteomics is currently one of the hottest topics, perfectly complementing the already more commonly used single cell transcriptomic studies, that will significantly contribute to several high impact publications in near future. The protein chemistry facility is currently among the top 5 groups worldwide in the field of single cell proteomics and already co-developed several innovative sample preparation workflows as well as significantly improved separation performance, sensitivity, and data acquisition for ultra-low input samples.

We aim to keep our innovations and success story ongoing as described above. This will clearly contribute to the success of the whole campus as all scientists will have access to the here developed techniques and we will tightly adjust our methodical advancements to the needs of basic research done on campus. On the long term this can only be ensured by highly qualified and trained staff scientists, which are permanently funded. Finding such trained employees is very time consuming. Introduction to such complex high-tech machines as mass spectrometers takes quite long and with every leaving student also know-how leaves the group. In conclusion, we aim to provide a permanently attractive working environment to keep basic staff and know-how within the group as long as possible and in conclusion speed up future method development.

News

Mar 20 2026

FWF Grant Supports New Single-Cell Multiomics Method

The Austrian Science Fund (FWF) has awarded a Stand Alone Grant to Manuel Matzinger and Viktoria Dorfer (Univ. of Applied Sciences Upper Austria) to develop a workflow that simultaneously analyses RNA and proteins from the same single cell. The project will use neural tube organoids to study how asymmetry arises during early nervous system development, with results made available via an open web application and R package.

Sep 29 2025

Manuel Matzinger Awarded APMA Society Medal for Single-Cell Proteomics Research

Manuel Matzinger, Deputy Head of the Proteomics Tech Hub, received the Society Medal of the Austrian Proteomics and Metabolomics Association (APMA) — the association's highest honour for early-career researchers. The award recognises his landmark Nature Methods publication describing a workflow that quantifies more than 5,000 proteins in a single cell. At the same conference, Matzinger was also elected to the APMA board.

Jan 16 2025

Unprecedented Protein Quantification in Single Cells Using Advanced Proteomics

Researchers at the Proteomics Tech Hub, in collaboration with Thermo Fisher Scientific and Nicolas Rivron's lab at IMBA, developed a single-cell proteomics workflow capable of quantifying up to 5,300 proteins per cell — published in Nature Methods. By optimising sample preparation, chromatography, and the novel Astral™ mass spectrometer, the team achieved resolution sufficient to detect cell-cycle stage and proteomic heterogeneity within seemingly uniform cell populations.

Aug 17 2023

€2.5M Funding for Single-Cell Proteomics

The Vienna BioCenter Core Facilities received €2.5 million from the Austrian Research Promotion Agency (FFG) to establish the Austrian Single-Cell Proteomics Platform (AT-SCP). The grant, led by Karl Mechtler and colleagues, funds state-of-the-art mass spectrometers and a picolitre-handling robotic system to push single-cell proteomics toward a robust, widely accessible collaboration service.

Apr 4 2023

Proteome Pioneer Award for Karl Mechtler

Karl Mechtler, Head of the Proteomics Tech Hub, became the first Austrian scientist to receive the prestigious Juan Pablo Albar Proteome Pioneer Award from the European Proteomics Association. The accolade recognises his decades of contributions to proteomics, both as a facility head and as a technology development leader.
